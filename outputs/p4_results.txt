Discussion
----------
(i) The baseline is 0.9815 (53 of 54 correct), so the threshold is 0.9630: PCA reaches
it at k = 3, the F-score filter at k = 5, and SFS only at k = 8. (ii) If the label-aware
methods made good use of the labels, they should beat PCA at small k, but the plot shows
the opposite: label-blind PCA is at or above both at every k, e.g. 0.9815 vs 0.9074
(F-score) and 0.8704 (SFS) at k = 3. This matches P3, where the high-variance directions
of the z-scored data happened to line up with class separation, which is a property of
this dataset and not something PCA guarantees in general. SFS, which uses the labels most
directly, does worst: it maximises CV accuracy on only 124 training points, so its picks
fit noise in the folds, as the drop from 0.8889 at k = 2 to 0.8704 at k = 3 suggests.
(iii) One test sample is worth 1/54 = 0.0185, and the binomial standard error at about
95% accuracy is sqrt(0.95 * 0.05 / 54) = 0.030, so gaps of one or two samples, like PCA
vs F-score at k = 5 and k = 8, are within noise. Only the big gaps at small k, such as the
6-sample difference between PCA and SFS at k = 3, give a ranking I would trust, and even
those come from a single fixed train/test split.
